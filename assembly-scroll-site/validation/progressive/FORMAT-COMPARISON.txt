LOCAL FORMAT COMPARISON, 2026-10-21

Decision: retain production WebP. AVIF 65 is a promising follow-up candidate,
but this desktop experiment does not establish mobile Safari or Android decoding performance.
No production images or videos were changed.

Coverage: 60 full-sequence encodings, 421 positions each, two orientations,
540p/720p/1080p. 120 browser cases passed across normal and constrained networking.
Constrained profile: 1.6 Mbps, 150 ms latency, 4x CPU slowdown.
Each case checks 11 random stops, 60 forward targets and 60 reverse targets.
Target updates coalesce while decoding, exact final stops and monotonic direction are asserted.
H.264 media timestamps were checked against the requested frame. HTTP range requests are supported.

Encoder: Pillow 13.0.0, WebP method 6, AVIF speed 8. FFmpeg 8.1.2,
libx264 fast preset, YUV420p, no B frames, CRF 18/23, GOP 1/6, faststart.
Browser: Chrome 153.0.8010.37. One case per candidate/profile, exploratory rather than statistical.

Visual review: format-detail-comparison.png compares chips, Wi-Fi and LoRa plugs,
and antenna labels against the original PNG. Label shapes and markings remain
recognizable in these selected 1080p crops. AVIF 50 and H.264 CRF23 show more
edge softening or artifacts in fine lines. AVIF 65 is a better candidate for a
future user review than selecting the smallest encoding solely by byte count.
The contact sheet is a spot check. It does not prove every label is readable on a phone.

WebP method 6 changes size modestly at quality 88. Quality 80 reduces bytes
but also reduces fidelity. AVIF provides larger savings with a decode tradeoff.
H.264 can be much smaller, especially with GOP6, but slow-network random seeks
are less predictable. A video transport would also require a separate scheduler.
