Fixes and regression evidence

1. Urgent frame scheduling
   A newest visible frame can release one obsolete, never-demanded neighboring
   transfer. Demanded transfers and completed neighbor downloads are protected.
   Cancellation cleanup releases the slot before another job starts.
   The browser regression holds four neighbors indefinitely and requires the
   Boards request to start before those neighbors are released. It passed in
   93 ms. The original audit reproduction delayed neighbors for 3.2 seconds and
   measured a 3,156 ms target-start delay, retained in baseline-jump.json.
   These controlled failure-injection cases demonstrate removal of queue
   blocking. They are not an ordinary-network performance benchmark.

2. Quality recovery
   Per-visit speculative network attempts are separate from recovery-round
   eligibility. A round measures at most three candidates. After the existing
   15-second cooldown, cached compressed images can be decoded again. Expired or
   aborted batches also receive a bounded retry opportunity.
   The unit regression fails five rounds, then promotes at the unchanged final
   pose. The browser regression observes at least four slow 720p probe rounds,
   removes an injected decode delay, and recovers scrolling quality from 540p
   through 720p to 1080p without moving or reloading. The same 720p URL is never
   requested twice. Cached probes do not count as background transfers.

3. Keyboard access
   Instructions close when focus leaves the disclosure. Tab and Shift+Tab within
   it keep it open. Escape closes it from anywhere on the page and returns focus
   to its summary only when focus was inside. The connector modal retains its
   native Escape and focus return.
   Browser checks cover 1440x900, 390x844, 320x568 and 568x320. They verify both
   focus state and hit-test visibility of the connector plus icon after closing.

4. Chapter navigation
   Direct fragments, chapter clicks, Back/Forward, reload and modified-click
   opening work. Saved progress is namespaced in history.state. Chapter clicks
   create history entries, while ordinary scrolling updates the current entry
   after a 600 ms pause. Reload restores the saved position or the linked chapter.
   Immediate reload before that pause can restore the previous saved position.
   Reduced-motion scrolling does not write history on every frame.
   Regression checks also cover an initially unfragmented page, external hash
   changes, same-chapter clicks, navigation with a connector modal open, reduced
   motion and Retry after an ordinary HTML fallback chapter selection.

Validation notes and limits

The broad traversal and continuous-scroll runs preceded the final change to
debounce history writes. That final change was covered by the complete targeted
regression rerun and the progressive-loading rerun. No loader changes followed
the broad traversal runs.

Two older test assumptions were updated: Retry now honors the chapter selected
in fallback HTML, and repeated same-value quality notifications are deduplicated
before asserting the 1080p to 720p to 540p sequence. The reload regression waits
for the documented paused-position save rather than assuming every frame writes
history. A separate regression asserts zero history writes during continuous
reduced-motion scrolling and one write after pausing.

Safari WebDriver could not establish a session. Its checks are unavailable, not
passed. No Safari settings were changed. Physical Android, iPhone and screen
reader tests were not repeated for this change. Mobile checks are Chrome
emulation, which does not prove behavior on physical devices or real networks.
